# Bullets (up to 6 — leave a title/body pair blank to hide that bullet)

bullet-1-title: Foundation models are usually the right starting point
bullet-1-body: For reasoning, generation, and anything open-ended — foundation models are fast to integrate, require no labeled data, and keep improving. If integration works well, that is the answer.

bullet-2-title: The trigger is data and stability
bullet-2-body: If the task is well-defined, the labeled data exists, and the requirements are unlikely to change month to month — that is when building your own model starts to make sense.

bullet-3-title: Cost and latency become important at scale
bullet-3-body: A fine-tuned small model can be 10–100× cheaper per call than a frontier model. At real production volume, that difference compounds quickly.

bullet-4-title: Some decisions are about control, not performance
bullet-4-body: Privacy constraints, compliance requirements, full auditability — when data cannot leave your infrastructure, a local or self-hosted model is the right architecture.

bullet-5-title: Custom models carry the same quality obligations
bullet-5-body: Test sets, scoring rubrics, production monitoring — these apply regardless of who trained the model. The quality discipline is just fully in your hands.
